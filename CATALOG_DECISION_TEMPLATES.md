# Developer catalog decision messages

Use these as starting points for a personal email or direct message after a bounded WAI review. They communicate an **editorial outcome**, not an automated submission status. `Accept for research`, a completed local draft, and an admin `Reviewed` stage are not permission to tell a developer their app has been listed. Ryan approves each final message before it is sent.

Read `CATALOG_REVIEW.md`, `CATALOG_DICTATED_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md`, the app's private research notes, and the current saved/public card before filling a template. Use the released product and dated evidence actually evaluated. Do not turn a decision message into a full QA report, a list of requested fixes, a score negotiation, or a sales pitch.

## Before sending either outcome

- Confirm the app name, decision, review dates, device/platform, country and access tier. Include a version only if it was actually captured. Say `version not recorded` internally rather than guessing it in the message.
- Identify the principal journey WAI tried and whether a workout was performed, merely logged, or only inspected. Select at most two or three material consumer-facing findings. Balance a decline with a genuine strength when the evidence supports one.
- Attribute current behavior to **WAI's tested path**, not every user, device, tier, or later release. Do not turn an unresolved cause into a diagnosed defect.
- Check the latest known product changes. If a later release was not retested, make the test date explicit and do not imply the finding still occurs. A developer's statement that a fix shipped is not a retest.
- Keep factual corrections distinct from a request for another full evaluation. Evidence-backed corrections may be submitted at any time through `https://workoutappindex.com/corrections`; they do not automatically change scores or trigger retesting.
- Do not include private notes, internal confidence labels, unpublished scores, a proposed public profile, or a paid-service offer. Payment does not purchase an editorial conclusion; developer correspondence alone is not independent product evidence. If a follow-up assessment product is approved later, describe it in a separate, clearly disclosed offer under its then-current policy.

## Outcome A: selected and publicly listed

**Send only after** Ryan has approved the exact saved card, used **Publish approved catalog**, and verified that the public profile is live. Do not use this template for an app merely accepted into research or marked `Reviewed` in admin.

```text
Subject: {{app_name}} is listed on Workout App Index

Hi {{developer_name}},

Thank you for submitting {{app_name}}. We have completed our bounded review and published its Workout App Index profile: {{public_profile_link}}.

We evaluated {{tested_journey}} on {{platform_and_device}} using {{access_tier}} during {{review_dates}}. {{one_sentence_on_a_supported_strength_and_who_it_suits}}

The profile also explains {{one_or_two_material_tradeoffs_or_limits}}. Our assessment is tied to the product and access we tested; it does not certify every feature, platform, or future release.

If a specific fact is inaccurate or later becomes outdated, please send the statement and supporting source through https://workoutappindex.com/corrections. We review factual corrections separately from a new full evaluation.

Thanks again,
Ryan
Workout App Index
```

The link and the claimed public status must be verified after publication. Do not call a listing an endorsement, guarantee, or certification. If there is a material commercial relationship relevant to the profile, follow the current disclosure policy on the profile as well as in communications.

## Outcome B: not selected for publication at this stage

**Send only after** Ryan has made the editorial decision on the bounded evidence. Do not say the app was rejected because a developer did not answer WAI, and do not suggest that rapid fixes or payment will secure a listing.

```text
Subject: Workout App Index review of {{app_name}}

Hi {{developer_name}},

Thank you for submitting {{app_name}}. We appreciated {{one_genuine_supported_strength}}. We have completed this round of review and decided not to publish a Workout App Index listing at this stage.

Our evaluation on {{platform_and_device}} using {{access_tier}} during {{review_dates}} covered {{tested_journey}}. The main consumer-facing reasons for our decision were {{finding_one}} and {{finding_two}}. {{Optional_third_finding_if_decisive}}

These are observations from the paths and release we tested, not conclusions about every feature or later update. We did not evaluate {{important_unassessed_scope}}. This round is now closed; WAI does not retest each subsequent fix as part of the same review.

If we have misstated a fact, you can send the specific correction and supporting evidence through https://workoutappindex.com/corrections. A correction is not a request for a new full evaluation.

Thank you again for sharing the app with us.

Best,
Ryan
Workout App Index
```

Use the smallest number of findings that explains the decision. If the barrier was lack of access or evidence, say that instead of claiming the underlying feature failed. Do not insert a waiting period, resubmission price, or promised reconsideration until WAI has approved and published those terms. Keep any private product-testing offer separate from this decision message.
