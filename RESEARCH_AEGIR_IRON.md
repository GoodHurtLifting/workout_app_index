## Owner's first session, October 14

- Onboarding: the owner liked the Viking/Nordic styling, privacy explanation, and immediate definitions of cycle, block, week, and session. The wizard offered strength, powerbuilding, powerlifting, and sport-specific functional paths. The owner chose strength and muscle, intermediate, three full-body days, heavy and volume days, six exercises per session, an eight-week cycle, and four-week blocks. The plan was produced without an excessive delay. These choices are an observed path, not a test of every wizard branch.
- Plan review: muscle-volume and week-layout previews appeared before building. The volume chart and later progression charts were difficult to interpret quickly. In the horizontally scrolling weekly layout, the frozen movement column appeared to overlap day content. The owner skipped long explanatory text. Exercise demos and target-muscle information were useful. Some prescriptions appeared to lack an upper rep-range bound; the reason is unknown. The terms "program" and "cycle" may not be used consistently enough for a new user.
- Workout: the session screen contained many sections in one vertical scroll. The first day appeared near the bottom of the program list; tapping Log opened near the bottom of that day's exercise list, requiring an upward scroll. The current lb/kg unit was not obvious. Auto rest timing was appreciated. The owner could not tell whether the interface was intended for per-set logging; marking sets and then an exercise complete required discovery. Controls for miss, band, cluster, EMOM, warm-up, AMRAP, and split were not explained in context. A finish warning said some prescribed sets were unlogged but did not identify them. This does not establish that per-set logging is absent or that every user will have the same difficulty.
- After completion: the app showed a basic workout summary, PRs, and a 12-week 1RM chart for squat that repeated the same value after only one squat session. The owner returned to Home easily and saw updated progress content. They liked the product and developer but would not personally choose it as a daily logger. Separate that preference from the broadly testable navigation and completion-cue issues.
- Settings follow-up: the owner found Captain Ægir, labeled as a free helper, and a setting to enter an estimated resistance for each color of band they own. That is a useful, potentially distinctive accommodation for band training, though its effect on prescriptions or logs has not been tested. Settings also showed the current unit as pounds and offered a kg/lb switch; the unit had not been clear on the workout screen. The optional Anthropic AI coach was offered at $4.99/month or $9.99/month with a higher monthly usage limit and no trial shown in that purchase flow. Separate paid coach-roster tools appear designed for coaches to add their own athletes and manage their workouts, with a free-trial offer. The owner did not find a flow for lifters to search for or choose a coach. This is a navigation observation, not proof that no such flow exists. The coach workflow and trial length remain untested; pricing may differ by region.
- Privacy and integrations follow-up: settings showed buttons labeled "Withdraw consent of stored health data" and "Delete stored data," plus an option to connect Strava. These are observed controls, not proof that withdrawal, deletion, or Strava synchronization works as described. The exact scope of "stored data" and whether withdrawal also stops future collection need checking.

## Coach programming and data portability

- Coach tools are more than roster management. The [official site](https://www.aegirlab.com/) describes a coach writing programs and meet plans, pushing them to athletes' accounts, and sending subsequent changes through the app. The athlete then performs and logs the work while the coach can review training and adjust the plan. WAI observed the invite connection and coach views, not this end-to-end program-delivery loop. Present delivery as a first-party documented capability until verified, while recognizing that it is central to the product's coach offering.
- The developer's stated design priority is continuity for lifters moving among strength sports or changing coaches. The official site describes importing prior programs, lifts, PRs, and meet results and exporting logged training data. This matters both to individual athletes who want to retain their history and to coaches onboarding clients with existing records, or building a roster without starting every athlete from zero. WAI has not performed an import or export. The available descriptions do not establish bulk import of an entire existing roster, roster-wide export, or what data either party retains after a coach-athlete relationship ends; clarify those boundaries before making a more specific public claim.
