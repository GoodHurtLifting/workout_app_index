# Ægir Iron: private initial assessment

Updated October 14, 2026 after one owner-run onboarding and workout. Submission `bZbRXp2hmnLHuuzqWLNF` is a developer claim, not independent evidence. This draft is for owner review, not an approved or public WAI listing. The matching internal Candidate identifier is `gir-iron`; the owner is comfortable leaving that hidden identifier as is.

## Preliminary scores

- Legit Score **73/100**, Low confidence: core execution 80, usability 60, reliability 60, value 90, support/privacy 75. The five criteria are equally weighted under `initial-v1`. One hands-on session supports the wizard's core program-building promise and the ability to complete a workout. The dense log and unclear completion cues lower usability. The thoughtful privacy explanation improves transparency, but actual data handling is unverified. Sync, coach workflow, and longer-term progression remain untested.
- Originality & Identity **74/100**, Clear identity: original mechanics 75, product point of view 80, visual identity 80, meaningful differentiation 75, defensibility 60. The owner directly observed a cohesive Nordic visual language that fits the strength-sport positioning. Distinctiveness of the mechanics relative to competitors remains untested.
- Fit: three distinct potential users need evaluation. A self-coached strength athlete gets periodized programming, detailed logging, and meet tools; an athlete already working with an Ægir coach can receive that coach's programs and meet plans; a strength coach can pay to manage a roster and follow athlete training. The coach-athlete workflow is a substantive part of the product's positioning, not merely a settings add-on, but WAI has tested only the solo path. All three groups need tolerance for a detailed interface; the owner's personal dislike of logging is a fit preference, not itself a product defect. This is not established as a marketplace for finding a coach.

## Owner's first session, October 14

- Onboarding: the owner liked the Viking/Nordic styling, privacy explanation, and immediate definitions of cycle, block, week, and session. The wizard offered strength, powerbuilding, powerlifting, and sport-specific functional paths. The owner chose strength and muscle, intermediate, three full-body days, heavy and volume days, six exercises per session, an eight-week cycle, and four-week blocks. The plan was produced without an excessive delay. These choices are an observed path, not a test of every wizard branch.
- Plan review: muscle-volume and week-layout previews appeared before building. The volume chart and later progression charts were difficult to interpret quickly. In the horizontally scrolling weekly layout, the frozen movement column appeared to overlap day content. The owner skipped long explanatory text. Exercise demos and target-muscle information were useful. Some prescriptions appeared to lack an upper rep-range bound; the reason is unknown. The terms "program" and "cycle" may not be used consistently enough for a new user.
- Workout: the session screen contained many sections in one vertical scroll. The first day appeared near the bottom of the program list; tapping Log opened near the bottom of that day's exercise list, requiring an upward scroll. The current lb/kg unit was not obvious. Auto rest timing was appreciated. The owner could not tell whether the interface was intended for per-set logging; marking sets and then an exercise complete required discovery. Controls for miss, band, cluster, EMOM, warm-up, AMRAP, and split were not explained in context. A finish warning said some prescribed sets were unlogged but did not identify them. This does not establish that per-set logging is absent or that every user will have the same difficulty.
- After completion: the app showed a basic workout summary, PRs, and a 12-week 1RM chart for squat that repeated the same value after only one squat session. The owner returned to Home easily and saw updated progress content. They liked the product and developer but would not personally choose it as a daily logger. Separate that preference from the broadly testable navigation and completion-cue issues.
- Settings follow-up: the owner found Captain Ægir, labeled as a free helper, and a setting to enter an estimated resistance for each color of band they own. That is a useful, potentially distinctive accommodation for band training, though its effect on prescriptions or logs has not been tested. Settings also showed the current unit as pounds and offered a kg/lb switch; the unit had not been clear on the workout screen. The optional Anthropic AI coach was offered at $4.99/month or $9.99/month with a higher monthly usage limit and no trial shown in that purchase flow. Separate paid coach-roster tools appear designed for coaches to add their own athletes and manage their workouts, with a free-trial offer. The owner did not find a flow for lifters to search for or choose a coach. This is a navigation observation, not proof that no such flow exists. The coach workflow and trial length remain untested; pricing may differ by region.
- Privacy and integrations follow-up: settings showed buttons labeled "Withdraw consent of stored health data" and "Delete stored data," plus an option to connect Strava. These are observed controls, not proof that withdrawal, deletion, or Strava synchronization works as described. The exact scope of "stored data" and whether withdrawal also stops future collection need checking.

## Evidence and discrepancies

- The [US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/%C3%A6gir-iron/id6763792846) and [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.aegirlab.app.twa) confirm live iOS and Android listings. Store descriptions of workouts, progression, offline use, and AI are written by the developer, not verified by Apple or Google. The [official site](https://www.aegirlab.com/) explicitly positions the app for self-coached lifters, athletes training under a coach, and coaches running rosters. It describes an invite-link connection, coach-pushed programs and meet plans, roster-wide recovery/workload views, free athlete accounts, and coach plans starting at $3.99 monthly with a 30-day trial. Those coach-side workflows and purchase gates still need hands-on checking.
- The submission says 600+ exercises; Google Play says more than 540; the current site says nearly 1,000. Avoid an exact library count until reconciled. The App Store lists English and says Designed for iPhone, whereas the site describes multiple language tools and iPad access. Test what is translated and whether the iPad layout is native or scaled.
- The [privacy policy](https://www.aegirlab.com/privacy/) describes Firebase, Vercel Analytics, Sentry, Stripe/RevenueCat, optional Anthropic use, and optional Apple Health and Strava links. It says the paid assistant can receive training context, while two free AI helpers use narrower inputs. This is a disclosure, not an independently verified data-flow audit. Do not repeat the submission's unqualified "no tracking SDKs" claim.
- Google Play declares collection of personal, health/fitness, and app-performance data; Apple's privacy section lists identity-linked health, payment, contact, user-content, identifier, and usage categories. These are developer-provided store disclosures, not independent verification.
