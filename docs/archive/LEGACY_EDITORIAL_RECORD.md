# Initial-v1 scoring rule

## Current public boundary — November 4, 2026

The owner clarified that Legit and Originality ratings must not be public, including historical expandable sections. Public pages and the catalog API no longer expose those ratings or offer scored editorial coverage. Historical rating data remains private and unchanged. Personalized preference Fit, sourced product information, and scoped firsthand observations remain. This instruction supersedes earlier transition language below that retained public ratings. No deployment-triggering push or rollout monitoring is part of this cleanup; hand off the verified local commit to the owner.

## Facts-first applicability — November 4, 2026

The initial-v1 formula and saved/public scores are unchanged. This rubric applies to evidence-supported scored evaluations, not every factual profile. Do not backfill numbers, use zero as an unknown score, or require unpaid testing merely to satisfy the current schema. Unknown information is not failure. The ordinary-profile research allowance is zero to approximately ten minutes of firsthand owner verification; selected deeper editorial work remains separately bounded.

The owner-approved [additive migration](IMPLEMENTATION_HISTORY.md) now supports source-based factual-profile approval and unrated public rendering. Records without an explicit factual mode retain their previous scored behavior. No existing score values are rewritten by this migration. Private testing does not purchase editorial selection or automatically reopen scoring. Historical expedited editorial-priority language below is superseded prospectively by the business plan; retain actual prior agreements for explicit owner disposition. Factual corrections remain free.

The five Legit Score criteria are equally weighted (20% each). Each criterion uses five-point increments; the displayed total is their rounded arithmetic mean. Use the same anchors for every app: 90-100 means demonstrated, consistently strong performance; 75-89 means strong observed or documented performance with limited issues; 60-74 means a credible offering with material observed issues; 40-59 means major observed limitations; below 40 means core failure or substantial negative evidence. Missing evidence is not treated as failure or an automatic point deduction. It does, however, prevent a dimension from reaching a band whose definition requires demonstrated or consistent performance. Compare candidates against the privately reassessed legacy entries under this rubric, not unrevised public legacy scores.

The criteria are: core execution (does the main training/logging promise work?), usability (can a person understand and complete the flow?), reliability (did the observed paths function without confirmed failures?), value (current free and paid boundaries relative to delivered utility), and support/privacy (published disclosures, visible controls, ordinary customer-facing support routes, and any response observed through those routes). A developer's response to WAI's editorial outreach is not customer support evidence and never earns bonus points. The last criterion does not certify legal compliance, backend data handling, or security. Public product claims and store disclosures inform the assessment; direct testing is distinguished from those claims.

Originality & Identity uses five equally weighted dimensions already represented in the catalog: original mechanics, product point of view, visual identity, meaningful differentiation, and defensibility. Its total is their rounded mean. A feature's mere existence is not proof of originality. A low-confidence originality score can move after direct competitor and hands-on testing.

## Repeatable scoring procedure

Use the [dictated product review protocol](LEGACY_EDITORIAL_RECORD.md) to separate documented claims, owner observations, developer reports, inferences, and unknowns before scoring. Score the product and tier actually evaluated. Record the evidence and one concrete reason for each dimension's band. Start from the band whose description fits the observed or documented behavior; use five-point increments for new criterion judgments rather than false one-point precision. Existing saved and public numbers do not change merely because this procedure is documented.

| Dimension | What earns a strong band | What moves it toward a lower band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core execution | The main promised training/planning/logging job can be completed in the evaluated path. | Material workaround, incomplete primary task, or observed core failure. |
| Usability | Starting, logging, finishing, and resuming are clear when those paths are encountered. | Repeated navigation friction, unexplained controls, or a blocked path to the next action. |
| Reliability | The tested paths function and retain the expected state. State their scope; an 85 or higher needs meaningful repeatability evidence, and 90-100 needs demonstrated consistency across representative sessions or workflows. | Reproducible errors, failed saves/sync in tested paths, crashes, or data loss. Untested long-term behavior is not a failure, but one successful path cannot establish consistency. |
| Value | Current price and free/paid boundaries are clear relative to the usable core job. | Misleading or confusing access gates, high cost for limited utility, or a paywall that blocks the promised path. |
| Support/privacy | Current public disclosures, visible controls, ordinary customer-facing support infrastructure, and any customer-support response actually observed are coherent. | Missing or conflicting material disclosures, ineffective observed controls, or a documented unresolved ordinary support interaction. No contact is neutral, not evidence of poor response. Editorial correspondence with a developer is excluded. This is not a security audit. |
| Original mechanics | A specific training or product mechanism differs meaningfully from relevant alternatives, with an evidence-backed comparison. | Familiar category mechanics or a novelty claim unsupported by a concrete mechanism. |
| Product point of view | Product decisions coherently serve an identifiable user and tradeoff. | Broad or conflicting promises with no clear intended training relationship. |
| Visual identity | Distinctive, coherent presentation supports the product's use and audience. | Generic, inconsistent, or obstructive presentation. Taste alone is not a high score. |
| Meaningful differentiation | A verified combination or execution gives a target user a reason to choose it over named peers. | Differences are minor, unverified, or only marketing language. |
| Defensibility | A substantiated mechanism, integrated workflow, community, data advantage, or other real obstacle makes replication meaningfully harder. | The offering appears straightforward to copy; founder effort or aesthetics alone do not establish a barrier. |

Use the existing global bands above for **each** dimension: 90-100 requires strong affirmative evidence for that dimension; 75-89 means the promised behavior works with limited issues; 60-74 means credible but materially limited; 40-59 means a major observed limitation; below 40 requires substantial negative evidence. A dimension cannot be raised or lowered just to make the overall total feel right. Missing evidence can limit an upward claim that requires demonstrated performance without being scored as a defect. A verified product improvement affects the relevant product dimension, usually usability or core execution; the developer's speed or friendliness in responding to WAI does not independently raise Support/privacy. Compare against named, relevant apps, not against an unrevised legacy public total. If evidence for a dimension is too thin to defend any band, keep the assessment internal and document the gap rather than inventing precision. The overall score is the rounded mean of the five dimensions, not an additional editorial override.

Record scoring date, evaluated platform/tier, evidence scope, named comparison set where used, and confidence separately from the number. A later observation changes a dimension only when it changes the evidence for that dimension; write down the old and new rationale. Do not silently rescore because an app received more attention. Internal confidence describes the evidence, not the product's quality.

### Confidence anchors

- **Low:** At most one principal journey on one platform was observed, or important workflows or repeatability remain untested. A documentation-only assessment is also Low.
- **Moderate:** The principal journey was completed; some repeat use, return behavior, or history was examined; and important commercial and policy facts were cross-checked. State any remaining material gaps.
- **High:** Multiple representative sessions or workflows provide meaningful repeatability evidence, and major claims have been independently checked. This should be uncommon for a bounded catalog review. It still does not imply every platform, feature, data flow, or long-term outcome was audited.

These are evidence-scope anchors, not a formula or a reward for time spent. More developer contact alone cannot raise confidence. If the evidence fits different anchors for different dimensions, use the lower overall confidence and explain the uneven scope.

## Editorial boundary

Developer answers may correct facts, but they do not set WAI scores. The owner reviews the exact evidence-backed card before approval; publication is a separate action. A paid expedited editorial retest uses this same rubric and approval path, with a fee fixed regardless of outcome. Private fix checks and audits buy developer-facing work and do not by themselves reopen listing consideration. Payment cannot buy a higher score, favorable conclusion, ranking, finder placement, or publication.

# Dictated product review protocol

## Scope update — November 4, 2026

Use this protocol for **selected hands-on editorial work** and agreed private testing, not as a mandatory checklist for every factual catalog profile. Ordinary research starts with public primary sources and zero to approximately ten minutes of owner verification when useful; unknowns remain unknown. See the dated override in [CATALOG_REVIEW.md](../CATALOG_WORKFLOW.md) and the governing [business plan](../../WAI_BUSINESS_PLAN.md).

For articles, comparisons, pricing research, alternatives, best-app articles, onboarding/paywall comparisons, and podcast/video source material, use [EDITORIAL_CONTENT_TEMPLATE.md](../EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md). Dictated notes are the primary evidence for the owner's experience. Preserve the words and context; do not embellish, generalize across untested tiers, or manufacture a firsthand verdict from documentation. App-store documentation is distinct from other official documentation; explicitly label conflicts and facts not established. Pricing-only research can be source-only and must say so.

The 60–90 minute limit below is for selected editorial sessions. Paid work follows its separately agreed one-hour or two-to-three-hour scope. Neither longer use nor a private engagement creates a publication entitlement. Scores are considered only when evidence can support all required dimensions; unscored factual profiles follow the source-based path in FACTUAL_PROFILE_MIGRATION.md without inventing scores.

Use the guide below for selected hands-on reviews and substantive hands-on reassessments. This is an interviewer guide for WAI, not a form for the owner or another publication gate. The owner may narrate in any order, by voice, during ordinary use. Do not require a new workout, a support request, or a return visit solely to complete this guide. Record `not observed` when appropriate. Refer to [the scoring rubric](LEGACY_EDITORIAL_RECORD.md) before proposing numbers.

## 1. Prepare before asking the owner

Research the official product site, current store listing in a named country, current pricing/free-tier terms, privacy policy, help/support material, and any relevant creator or program source. Record URL, access date, region, and exactly which claim each page supports. A developer submission is a lead, not verification. Make a short claim inventory, flag conflicts, and identify only the high-impact gaps that owner use could clarify. Do not ask the owner to rediscover facts that are already documented.

The pre-use brief should take at most a few lines: the product's stated job, its apparent programming model, the access tier, and two or three specific questions worth noticing. These are prompts, not a testing assignment.

### Normal-review testing limit

Budget approximately 60-90 minutes of hands-on use for a selected hands-on editorial review, separate from public-source research and drafting. Follow one primary-user journey, allow one reasonable retry after a barrier, and try one obvious alternative route if one exists. Stop when the main journey and material tradeoff are understood, or when the timebox and retry/alternative limit are reached. The owner may continue using an app voluntarily, but extra use is not a publication prerequisite and does not automatically make the review a paid audit.

Do not troubleshoot accounts beyond that ordinary retry, coordinate special tester access, test unreleased builds, or help a developer repair a blocked workflow as part of catalog research. If a consequential feature remains inaccessible, record the attempted path and disclose the limitation; do not claim the feature is absent or working. A subscription or access barrier may be described from current public terms without purchasing access solely to finish a catalog card.

## 2. Listen for the same seven checkpoints

Let the owner dictate naturally. Track these checkpoints silently and ask a short follow-up only when a material field remains unclear. Ask what happened, not for a 0-100 rating or a feature count. Capture device, app version if known, date, country/store, access tier, goal, and whether a workout was actually performed or only explored/simulated.

| Checkpoint | Follow-up only if missing | Primary catalog use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry and access | What could you do before a paywall or trial? Did permissions or setup prevent reaching training? | Price, usable free tier, value, access caveat |
| Program and choice | What plan or session did it produce, and what did you still have to choose or build? | Complete program, library, authorship, planning style, choice load, fit |
| Workout and **logging** | How did you start, enter a set, change it, and finish? Which steps felt clear or cumbersome? | Core execution, usability, fast-logging claim, features |
| Adaptation and continuity | What changed after performance, a missed session, or a return? Was the next action visible or did you have to find it? | Adaptive programming, progression, continuity, tradeoff |
| Feedback and history | What did the app show after the session, and could you find the prior sets or progress you expected? | History/analytics features, usability, value |
| Support and privacy | If you actually sought help or encountered data controls, what happened? Otherwise, leave support response and real data handling untested. | Support/privacy rationale and any concrete caveat |
| Identity and fit | What was genuinely distinctive in use, what was familiar, and who would find the tradeoff worthwhile? | Best for, not for, originality dimensions, central tradeoff |

The owner does not need to cover every checkpoint in one session. Do not interpret silence as a failed feature. A simulated log is not a completed workout. A proposed AI change is not an applied change until observed. A visible button is not proof that the underlying operation succeeds. Stop at the normal-review limit rather than extending testing merely to fill every checkpoint.

## 3. Reconcile claims and observations

For every material statement in the draft card, keep a compact evidence record: claim, source class, date/version/region/tier, what was actually observed or documented, conflict or limitation, and affected catalog field. Use these classes:

- **Owner observed:** action and result in the tested product. Preserve the test context; distinguish a one-off observation from a repeated defect.
- **Officially documented:** current site, store, policy, or help-page statement. This verifies what the publisher says, not necessarily product behavior.
- **Developer reported:** submission or correspondence. Use as a question to verify, never as independent corroboration.
- **Editorial inference:** WAI's fit, tradeoff, comparison, or score judgment. State the underlying observations and comparison set.
- **Unresolved:** missing, stale, contradictory, or untested. Qualify or omit the public claim; do not silently turn it into `false` or into a confirmed feature.

When sources conflict, prefer a directly observed current path for that device/tier, then current store or policy language for platform/pricing/privacy statements. Do not generalize one owner's experience to all platforms, countries, or users. A focused factual clarification may be requested when useful, but developer participation is not required. If an answer does not arrive, qualify or omit the claim rather than delaying a bounded review. A developer answer can correct facts but cannot set a WAI score.

### Developer interaction boundary

Developer participation is not required before publication. WAI does not provide routine progress updates, advance score negotiation, or prepublication approval. Developers may submit documented factual corrections and material product updates through the site's correction channel. Editorial calls or email exchanges are not customer-support tests and do not earn Support/privacy points. If a developer changes the live product, score the verified product behavior in the relevant dimension; record the version/date and do not award a separate responsiveness bonus. When a bounded evaluation ends without a scored public entry, record the owner outcome in admin and draft the personal developer message using Outcome B in `CATALOG_DECISION_TEMPLATES.md`. Keep the message tied to the tested release and at most three grouped material findings; do not send it automatically or treat developer contact as a retest.

## 4. Translate to catalog fields consistently

Populate `type`, `description`, `bestFor`, `caveat`, `goals`, `level`, `platforms`, and the training relationship from the reconciled record, not from the developer's marketing category alone. State the decisions the app removes and those the user still owns. Choose one primary planning style and only documented or observed secondary styles. Do not label initial workout generation as ongoing adaptive programming.

Finder-sensitive labels require consistent meanings:

| Label or flag | Use only when evidence supports |
| --- | --- |
| Complete program | A coherent multi-session training path, not just one generated workout. |
| Program library / Expert programs | Multiple selectable reusable programs; `Expert programs` additionally needs identifiable human authorship. |
| Adaptive programming | The plan or upcoming sessions respond to performance or changing constraints, not merely to initial questionnaire answers. |
| Automatic progression | The product proposes or applies future loads, reps, or difficulty from prior performance; document whether approval is required. |
| Fast logging | A tested set-entry flow that remains quick and intelligible during a workout; do not infer it from a store adjective. |
| Detailed analytics | Interpretable trends or analyses beyond a basic workout-history list. |
| Offline use | Explicit current documentation or a direct offline test; do not infer from local caching. |
| Human coaching / Community | Actual human coach interaction / user-to-user activity, not an AI assistant or a founder's public forum alone. |
| Usable free tier | The core advertised job remains usable without a time-limited trial or mandatory payment. Record which program features are free separately. |

Use the exact finder-sensitive labels in `lib/matching.ts` when supported; keep other notable features descriptive rather than forcing them into a matching label. Price, platform, equipment, AI role, complete-program, library, adaptive, and free-tier flags can exclude an app from finder results, so do not guess them.

## 5. Score and hand off

For each of the five Legit and five Originality dimensions, record the band chosen, the specific supporting and limiting evidence, comparison apps where relevant, and confidence. Apply the same rubric each time. Do not ask the owner to supply numbers. An unknown lowers confidence or narrows the claim; it is not an automatic numeric penalty, but it cannot justify a band that requires demonstrated or repeated performance. Equally, a marketing claim alone does not demonstrate excellent execution. Use the Low/Moderate/High anchors in the scoring rubric. Keep scoring independent of developer cooperation, paid audits, and publication decisions.

Keep an internal decision row for each dimension: `dimension | proposed score | decisive evidence IDs | counterevidence | why this band rather than the adjacent band | confidence`. If a later observation changes a number, append the new reason and date instead of silently rewriting the old judgment. Use relevant apps as behavioral comparators, but do not copy their unrevised public totals.

The seven checkpoints are coverage prompts, **not** seven automatically verified sections. The old manual `verifiedSections`/`totalSections` values are retired from the editor and public screens; they remain in stored records only for compatibility. Do not use those values to claim completeness or raise a score. A concise, qualified card can be ready without every optional feature being tested; a material unresolved finder fact must be sourced, qualified, or omitted.

Prepare dated, claim-specific source records locally alongside the research draft. Saving a private card or accepting a local proposal attaches only those vetted records that are not already present. Do not convert a submission, an unvisited link, or an older preliminary-catalog seed into rechecked evidence. Existing source records are not overwritten. The owner reviews the card and source log; source entry is not another routine owner task. Missing support is resolved in research or the public claim is narrowed, not by asking the owner to fill in evidence fields.

Before presenting a proposed card, check that every finder-sensitive flag and public claim has an evidence path; the score totals match their dimensions; observed defects are not overstated; and important unknowns are visible. Show the owner one concise card and the material unresolved questions. Existing saved admin edits are accepted field by field, not overwritten. Owner review and public publication remain separate. Internal confidence labels are not developer-facing sales language.

This protocol does not rescore or republish existing apps automatically. Apply it to new research and to older entries when they are next substantively reviewed.

### Reusable structure
