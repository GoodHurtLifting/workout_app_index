# Firsthand coverage, source packs and developer messages

Current November 13, 2026. This combines the useful current parts of the dictated review protocol, editorial content template and decision templates. Ordinary factual catalog research follows [Catalog workflow](CATALOG_WORKFLOW.md); it does not require deep testing, a subjective score or a developer conversation. Earlier scoring/correspondence records remain [historical](archive/LEGACY_EDITORIAL_RECORD.md).

## Listen to actual experience

For selected editorial testing, prepare a brief from current official/store/pricing/privacy/help sources: stated job, programming model, tier and two or three useful uncertainties. Let Ryan dictate in any order. Track entry/access, program/choices, workout/logging, adaptation/continuity, feedback/history, support/privacy when actually encountered, and meaningful differences/fit in the background. Ask short follow-ups only for material ambiguities; do not send a form or demand extra workouts to finish a checklist.

A normal selected editorial session can be bounded to roughly 60–90 minutes of hands-on use, separate from research/drafting. Allow one reasonable retry and an obvious alternative after a material barrier. Agreed paid work follows its own signed duration and scope, rather than a historic package timebox. Do not troubleshoot accounts, test unreleased builds or help repair a product as routine catalog research. Record an inaccessible path as a limitation rather than a confirmed failure or absence.

Preserve device, platform, country/store, date, known version and access tier. Distinguish a physical workout, simulated log and exploration. A button is not proof its operation works, a suggested change is not an applied adaptation, and silence is not a failed feature. A support exchange for editorial clarification is not an ordinary customer-support test. Reported fixes remain unverified until checked; later builds may differ.

## Reusable source pack

Use the following structure internally, adapting it to the work. It is not an owner questionnaire.

1. **Commission and consumer question:** intended reader/decision, format, products, selection criteria and documented demand if available. State any commissioned scope and relationship.
2. **Context per product:** platform/device, version or not recorded, date, country/store, access tier/pricing and actual-workout/simulated/explored/not-tested status.
3. **Natural notes:** narration in sequence with session markers; preserve meaning and context.
4. **Observation log:** evidence ID; workflow/action; actual result; repeated or one-off; consumer impact; what was not established.
5. **Claim reconciliation:** claim; existing field; evidence class; URL or observation ID; checked date; region/tier/version; supported value; conflict/unknown.
6. **Reasoned interpretation:** supported strengths, friction, fit and tradeoffs, with evidence IDs and named comparison set. Separate editorial inference from observed facts.
7. **Public draft:** concise consumer-focused copy, tested scope/untested areas, source links, relevant disclosures and no subjective numeric ratings.
8. **Handoff state:** local draft, exact owner-approved version and separately published state. No automatic publication or messages.

Use the six catalog evidence classes consistently. Pricing includes currency, period, annual versus monthly terms, trials/free limits and regional/date caveats. Do not generalize a documented or observed feature across untested platforms, tiers or countries. Detailed chronology and contact data stay out of public copy; completed client reports and correspondence stay out of Git.

## Adapt the output to the question

| Format | Required framing |
| --- | --- |
| Hands-on review | Tested journey, supported strengths/friction, intended user, limits and dated conclusion |
| App-versus-app | Same consumer task, separate scope per app, relevant tradeoffs and asymmetric evidence limits |
| Pricing/free tier | Dated regional primary-source comparison; explicitly source-only when not tested |
| Alternatives or best apps | Defined need/audience, eligibility, comparison set, evidence and limits; no universal or paid winner |
| Onboarding/paywalls | Attempted access, trial conversion, purchase boundary and actual next step; no guessed backend diagnosis |
| Podcast/video source pack | Evidence-linked outline and demonstration plan; no invented footage, quote or workout |

Credit successful product differences with a concrete explanation: what differs, compared with what, who benefits, the tradeoff and what worked in the tested scope. This requires editorial judgment; it is not an automated Originality score, finder bonus or paid benefit. Reusing evidence does not expand what was tested. These source-pack formats do not imply a general article/podcast/video publishing system is implemented.

## Draft-only developer messages

Only draft a personal message when Ryan requests one. Check the saved owner decision and actual publication state first. No submission, review-stage or payment change authorizes sending. Do not contact developers automatically, negotiate scores, request prepublication approval or use continued publication as leverage for a response. Keep messages tied to the tested release, with at most three grouped findings, relevant limits and the free corrections route. Do not append a sales pitch, checkout link or speculative retest promise.

**Factual profile published:** “{{App}} has a factual profile on Workout App Index: {{verified link}}. It draws on {{dated public-source scope}}. {{Precisely scoped firsthand contribution, only if any.}} If a fact is inaccurate or changes, please submit the statement and supporting source at https://workoutappindex.com/corrections.” Do not call it an endorsement/certification or imply hands-on testing that did not happen.

**Completed firsthand work:** state the actual disposition first; describe platform/tier/version/date and attempted journeys; give up to three observed consumer impacts; state important untested areas and later-release limits; offer free factual corrections. If unlisted, distinguish ineligibility, unresolved access and an owner editorial decision. Paid listing non-delivery/ineligibility follows the current full-refund rule, separately from private findings. Do not reuse retired “no scored entry” or six-month/paid-retest boilerplate for new factual listings.

**Already-public findings follow-up:** describe the tested scope and findings, ask only an essential unresolved factual question and state the actual continuing-publication position. A requested fix is not a guaranteed ongoing evaluation. Developer participation is optional. Clarify any request for correspondence/findings/profile/whole-entry privacy; Ryan approves actual public changes. Do not invent a public privacy note before an explicit documented request, delete historical evidence or reinterpret a privacy request as product failure. App-specific historical owner instructions remain historical records and must be checked when handling that app.

For commissioned deliverables, use the agreed scope, factual revision process, permissions and disclosure; do not substitute a routine catalog message for a signed agreement. See [Commercial scope template](COMMERCIAL_SCOPE_TEMPLATE.md).
