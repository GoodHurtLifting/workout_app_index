## Initial-v1 scoring rule

The five Legit Score criteria are equally weighted (20% each). Each criterion uses five-point increments; the displayed total is their rounded arithmetic mean. Use the same anchors for every app: 90-100 means demonstrated, consistently strong performance; 75-89 means strong evidence with limited issues; 60-74 means a credible offering with material issues or untested areas; 40-59 means major observed limitations; below 40 means core failure or substantial negative evidence. An untested feature is not treated as working; a provisional score has Low or Moderate confidence rather than implied certainty. These initial scores are not comparable to the original 36 until those entries are reassessed under the same rubric.

The criteria are: core execution (does the main training/logging promise work?), usability (can a person understand and complete the flow?), reliability (repeatable sessions, stable data/history/sync), value (current free and paid boundaries relative to delivered utility), and support/privacy (transparency, controls, support, and data practices). The last criterion does not certify legal compliance or security. Public product claims and store disclosures inform the assessment; direct testing is distinguished from those claims.

Originality & Identity uses five equally weighted dimensions already represented in the catalog: original mechanics, product point of view, visual identity, meaningful differentiation, and defensibility. Its total is their rounded mean. A feature's mere existence is not proof of originality. A low-confidence originality score can move after direct competitor and hands-on testing.

- Provisional Originality & Identity **78/100**, Distinctive: original mechanics 84, product point of view 80, visual identity 68, meaningful differentiation 86, defensibility 72.
- Fit: lifters comfortable with detailed training conversations who want a program and live set-by-set adjustments inside the log. Poor fit for someone avoiding AI or wanting an immediate, low-friction first workout.
- Evidence: the owner generated a 5x5 rebuild with recomposition accessories and completed workouts. The plan, warm-ups, logging, and load feedback worked well. Onboarding took about 15 minutes; a proposed 20-pound increase, adjustment flow, rest-timer reset, and navigation after the first workout raised usability questions. In workout two, AI feedback called for a change to "set 2" when two preceding warm-up sets made that appear to refer to the fourth set shown. The intended target was unclear; we have not established that Aldo changed the wrong load. The owner later found full-screen iPad mode, so the earlier black-border critique is not retained as a defect. The [US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aldo-coach-ai-workout-plan/id6802822375) currently identifies a free iPhone/Apple Watch app, 18+ age rating, and AI-driven program and set adjustments. Its privacy disclosures are the developer's statements, not an independent audit. The [developer site](https://aldo-coach.app/) and [privacy policy](https://aldo-coach.app/privacy) were part of the earlier research but were not retrievable for a fresh check today.
- Before owner approval: complete more workouts, check how suggested loads affect subsequent sessions and recovery days, and verify whether AI set numbers include warm-ups and point to the correct working sets. Retest post-workout navigation and timer behavior, confirm current free limits and countries, and recheck the privacy policy and exact AI-off behavior. Ask the developer about the Yahoo confirmation-email failure the owner observed, model-version claims, and the browser version.
