# Initial-v1 scoring rule

The five Legit Score criteria are equally weighted (20% each). Each criterion uses five-point increments; the displayed total is their rounded arithmetic mean. Use the same anchors for every app: 90-100 means demonstrated, consistently strong performance; 75-89 means strong observed or documented performance with limited issues; 60-74 means a credible offering with material observed issues; 40-59 means major observed limitations; below 40 means core failure or substantial negative evidence. An untested feature is not treated as working, but a missing long-term test is not itself negative evidence. It lowers confidence, not the numeric score. Compare candidates against the privately reassessed legacy entries under this rubric, not unrevised public legacy scores.

The criteria are: core execution (does the main training/logging promise work?), usability (can a person understand and complete the flow?), reliability (did the observed paths function without confirmed failures?), value (current free and paid boundaries relative to delivered utility), and support/privacy (published disclosures, visible controls, stated support, and any response WAI directly observed). The last criterion does not certify legal compliance, backend data handling, or security. Public product claims and store disclosures inform the assessment; direct testing is distinguished from those claims.

Originality & Identity uses five equally weighted dimensions already represented in the catalog: original mechanics, product point of view, visual identity, meaningful differentiation, and defensibility. Its total is their rounded mean. A feature's mere existence is not proof of originality. A low-confidence originality score can move after direct competitor and hands-on testing.

## Repeatable scoring procedure

Use the [dictated product review protocol](CATALOG_DICTATED_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md) to separate documented claims, owner observations, developer reports, inferences, and unknowns before scoring. Score the product and tier actually evaluated. Record the evidence and one concrete reason for each dimension's band. Start from the band whose description fits the observed or documented behavior; use five-point increments for new criterion judgments rather than false one-point precision. Existing saved and public numbers do not change merely because this procedure is documented.

| Dimension | What earns a strong band | What moves it toward a lower band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core execution | The main promised training/planning/logging job can be completed in the evaluated path. | Material workaround, incomplete primary task, or observed core failure. |
| Usability | Starting, logging, finishing, and resuming are clear when those paths are encountered. | Repeated navigation friction, unexplained controls, or a blocked path to the next action. |
| Reliability | The tested paths function and retain the expected state. State their scope. | Reproducible errors, failed saves/sync in tested paths, crashes, or data loss. Untested long-term behavior lowers confidence, not points. |
| Value | Current price and free/paid boundaries are clear relative to the usable core job. | Misleading or confusing access gates, high cost for limited utility, or a paywall that blocks the promised path. |
| Support/privacy | Current public disclosures, support route, visible controls, and any support response actually observed are coherent. | Missing or conflicting material disclosures, ineffective observed controls, or a documented unresolved support interaction. No contact is neutral, not evidence of poor response. This is not a security audit. |
| Original mechanics | A specific training or product mechanism differs meaningfully from relevant alternatives, with an evidence-backed comparison. | Familiar category mechanics or a novelty claim unsupported by a concrete mechanism. |
| Product point of view | Product decisions coherently serve an identifiable user and tradeoff. | Broad or conflicting promises with no clear intended training relationship. |
| Visual identity | Distinctive, coherent presentation supports the product's use and audience. | Generic, inconsistent, or obstructive presentation. Taste alone is not a high score. |
| Meaningful differentiation | A verified combination or execution gives a target user a reason to choose it over named peers. | Differences are minor, unverified, or only marketing language. |
| Defensibility | A substantiated mechanism, integrated workflow, community, data advantage, or other real obstacle makes replication meaningfully harder. | The offering appears straightforward to copy; founder effort or aesthetics alone do not establish a barrier. |

Use the existing global bands above for **each** dimension: 90-100 requires strong affirmative evidence for that dimension; 75-89 means the promised behavior works with limited issues; 60-74 means credible but materially limited; 40-59 means a major observed limitation; below 40 requires substantial negative evidence. A dimension cannot be raised or lowered just to make the overall total feel right. Compare against named, relevant apps, not against an unrevised legacy public total. If evidence for a dimension is too thin to defend any band, keep the assessment internal and document the gap rather than inventing precision. The overall score is the rounded mean of the five dimensions, not an additional editorial override.

Record scoring date, evaluated platform/tier, evidence scope, named comparison set where used, and confidence separately from the number. A later observation changes a dimension only when it changes the evidence for that dimension; write down the old and new rationale. Do not silently rescore because an app received more attention. Internal confidence describes the evidence, not the product's quality.

## Editorial boundary

Developer answers may correct facts, but they do not set WAI scores. The owner reviews the exact evidence-backed card before approval; publication is a separate action. Any paid private audit conversation is separate from these scores and from listing eligibility.
